Ghostty announces plans to leave GitHub after repeated disruptions

The project's creator says reliability has become a daily obstacle. The move is planned, not yet a completed migration.

Event date: 28 April 2026

Git
Written and published 10 November 2026. The date above refers to the event or measurement period, not the original publication of this article.

A notable decision

On 28 April 2026, Ghostty creator Mitchell Hashimoto announced plans to move the terminal project's primary development away from GitHub. He described near-daily problems with outages that disrupted reviews, automated workflows and routine development.

This was not presented as a completed migration. The plan was to move gradually while keeping a read-only mirror on GitHub, and the destination was still being discussed.

Why reliability matters

A development platform is more than somewhere to store code. Teams depend on pull requests, discussions, automation and deployment tooling to coordinate work. If those services repeatedly fail, the impact can be felt across the whole delivery process.

Hashimoto's frustration was notable partly because he had used GitHub for more than eighteen years. The issue was not a lack of affection for the product; it was an inability to get work done reliably.

The business lesson

The same principle applies to websites and digital services. A familiar platform is not automatically the best long-term fit if reliability, control or support stops meeting your needs.

Plan for resilience: keep backups, understand where your code and data live, and avoid making your business impossible to move when a supplier is no longer suitable.
